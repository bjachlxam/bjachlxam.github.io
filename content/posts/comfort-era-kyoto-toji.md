Second entry in the [hotel series](/categories/hotel-reviews/), and this time
we're in Kyoto. **Comfort Hotel ERA Kyoto Toji** is a Choice Hotels property
that opened in 2021 and was relaunched under the ERA brand in September 2023 —
182 rooms across 8 floors, sitting in the quiet streets south of Kyoto
Station, right by the famous Toji Temple and its five-story pagoda.

It's now my default recommendation for Kyoto, for one headline reason that
anyone who has traveled in Japan will immediately understand: **the rooms are
big**.

## The location: one train stop — or a short walk — from Kyoto Station

The hotel's doorstep is **Kintetsu Toji Station, a 3-minute walk away**.
From there it's exactly **one stop to Kyoto Station** (about 4 minutes on the
Kintetsu line) — and if you're traveling light, Kyoto Station itself is close
enough to reach on foot in roughly 20 minutes.

Buses along the main roads cover the rest of the city conveniently, which in
Kyoto matters: the bus network reaches most of the temples and districts you
actually came for, and the stops near the hotel make it an easy routine.

You also get a landmark for free: the hotel is essentially next door to **Toji
Temple**, the UNESCO World Heritage site with Kyoto's tallest pagoda. An
evening walk around the temple grounds when the crowds are gone is the kind
of thing you can't buy back later.

## The rooms: genuinely big (for Japan)

Let's put numbers on it: standard doubles here run around 15 m², and the twin
rooms about **23 m²** — which in a country where budget hotel rooms are
routinely half that feels almost decadent. The photo below is the room type I
stayed in (a triple):

![Triple room at Comfort Hotel ERA Kyoto Toji](/images/comfort-era-kyoto-cover.jpg)
*One of the triple rooms at Comfort Hotel ERA Kyoto Toji. Photo from the
hotel's official website (Choice Hotels Japan).*

Three proper beds, space to actually open three suitcases, and the
comfortable beds and decent bathrooms the reviews keep mentioning. For solo
travelers: book the twin or triple instead of squeezing into a 12 m² double —
the price gap is smaller than the comfort gap.

## The neighborhood

South of Kyoto Station is quiet at night — this is a residential side of the
city, not a nightlife district. But "quiet" doesn't mean "inconvenient":
within a few minutes' walk you'll find izakayas for dinner, convenience stores
for breakfast supplies, and fast food when you're done pretending. It's the
balance I want after a day of temple-hopping: calm for sleeping, everything
edible within reach.

## Verdict

**Comfort Hotel ERA Kyoto Toji** is the easy answer if you want: a spacious
room by Japanese standards, one-stop access to Kyoto Station, a quiet street
with food and convenience around the corner, and prices that undercut the
stationside hotels. For temple-heavy itineraries, the Toji Temple location is
a quiet bonus. I'll keep this post updated if anything changes.
