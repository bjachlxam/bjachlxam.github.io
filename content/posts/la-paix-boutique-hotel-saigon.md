This is the first post in what I expect will be a running series: places I
actually recommend staying, from the point of view of someone who cares about
three things — location, honest value, and a room you can work in. First up:
a small boutique hotel in Saigon's District 1.

## The location is the whole point

District 1 is where most people want to be in Ho Chi Minh City, and La Paix
sits about as centrally as it gets: roughly 500 meters from Union Square,
a short walk to the Saigon Opera House and City Hall, and close enough to
Ben Thanh's street food scene that dinner is a stroll, not a mission. The
location score on Booking sits at **9.4** — and in a city where traffic can
eat an hour of your day, "walkable" is worth real money.

Tan Son Nhat International Airport is about 6 km away. A Grab ride will
usually be the sanest option; the hotel can also arrange an airport shuttle
for a fee if you'd rather have someone waiting with a sign.

## The rooms: renovated, quiet, and workable

The rooms were renovated recently, and it shows: big comfortable beds, hot
water that actually stays hot, and clean lines throughout. You get a choice of
Superior Double, Superior Twin, Deluxe Double with an extra-large bed, or a
Family Room if you're traveling as a small group.

I stayed in the Deluxe Double — this is the room type, possibly the very room:

![The Deluxe Double room at La Paix Sài Gòn](/images/la-paix-room.jpg)

For the working nomads: rooms come with a **work desk, a sofa, air
conditioning, and free WiFi**, plus windows that open onto the city. It's not
a coworking space — but for emails, calls, and an afternoon of writing, it
holds up fine.

One practical detail from the fine print: an extra bed costs about **VND
350,000 (~$14) per night**, and check-in starts at 2 PM with a minimum
check-in age of 18.

## The people

If you read through guest reviews, two things come up again and again: the
location, and the **staff**. Recent guests have called out team members by
name for going out of their way — help with luggage, honest local advice,
the kind of small favors that don't show up in a star rating. There's a
24-hour front desk, a concierge, and a tour desk if you need bookings arranged.

I'll also give them credit for something rarer: the housekeeping team
includes hearing-impaired staff, and the hotel clearly makes inclusive
hiring work. Your money supports a workplace that does a little good, which
I'm happy about.

## The honest fine print

- It's a **boutique hotel, not a resort** — no pool, no gym, no buffet
  ballroom. If you need those, book one of the international chains a few
  streets over, at roughly double the price.
- Rates move with the season; check current prices at the link below. With a
  Booking **Genius discount** it often comes out even friendlier.
- Breakfast options nearby are better than most in-house spreads anyway —
  you're in District 1; go eat pho on the sidewalk like a local.
